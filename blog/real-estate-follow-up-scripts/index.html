Real estate lead follow-up scripts for text and email

Good real estate follow-up scripts do one job: get a reply. Not a pitch, not a list of your awards, just an easy question the lead wants to answer. Below are copy-ready texts and emails for the most common lead types, and the 30-day plan to send them on.

By Aftab Ahmed · October 5, 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways
  • The best first follow-up text is short, personal and ends with one easy question, not a pitch.
  • Reply within minutes: an automated first text can go out while you are still at a showing.
  • Use different scripts for portal buyers, open house visitors and seller valuation leads, because they want different things.
  • Most leads aren't ready to move yet, so plan months of light, useful follow-up instead of three calls and done.
  • Collect consent to text, include an opt-out, and keep every message fair-housing safe.

Real estate follow-up scripts fail for two reasons: they arrive too late, and they ask for too much. "Hi, I'm Jane with XYZ Realty, let me know if you'd like to schedule a showing or discuss your home search!" gives the lead nothing to respond to. "Hi Mark, saw you looked at 42 Oak St. Are you hoping to move this year or just getting a feel for prices?" does.

Use the scripts below as starting points. Swap in your own voice, keep them short, and send the first one within minutes of the enquiry.

What makes a real estate follow-up script work?

Four things: speed, a personal detail, one question, and a reason to reply. Everything else is optional.

  • Speed. The first message should land while the lead is still on the listing page. Minutes, not hours.
  • A personal detail. The address, the neighborhood or the price range they looked at. It proves a real person read their enquiry.
  • One question. Easy to answer with a word or two: timing, area, or whether they've seen it in person.
  • A reason to reply. Something useful: similar homes, an off-market option, the price history.

Leave out your company slogan, a list of services and anything that sounds like a mass text. And keep scripts fair-housing safe: talk about the home, the area's features and the buyer's stated needs, never about who lives in a neighborhood.

What should the first text to a new real estate lead say?

Name, the property or area they asked about, and one easy question. That's it. Here's the version I'd start with for almost any buyer lead:

First text (any buyer lead)
"Hi Mark, it's Jane from Oak Realty. Saw your question about 42 Oak St. Are you looking to move in the next few months, or still exploring? Either is fine, I can send you what fits."

If they reply, ask the next question: area, budget, must-haves, financing. If they don't, the plan below takes over.

Scripts for portal, open house and seller leads

Portal leads (Zillow, Realtor.com and similar)

These buyers often enquire on several homes and several agents at once. Be first and be specific.

Portal lead, minute 1
"Hi Priya, Jane here with Oak Realty. 18 Maple Ave is still available. Would you like to see it this week, or should I send a few similar homes nearby first?"
Portal lead, day 2 (no reply)
"Hi Priya, quick one: 18 Maple has had a few showings. Want me to check if the sellers are open to offers yet? Happy to send the price history too."

Open house visitors

They met you in person, so mention something from the visit. Send it the same evening.

Open house, same evening
"Hi Tom, thanks for stopping by 7 Cedar Ln today. You mentioned wanting a bigger yard. Want me to send a couple of homes with more outdoor space in the same school district?"

Seller and home valuation leads

Online valuation leads are often curious, not committed. Lead with value and keep the ask small.

Valuation lead, minute 1
"Hi Linda, Jane from Oak Realty. Your home value estimate for 210 Birch St is on its way. Online estimates can be off by a fair bit. Would a quick, no-pressure price range based on recent sales on your street be helpful?"
Valuation lead, day 3
"Hi Linda, two homes near Birch St sold this month. Want the details? Useful to know even if you're not planning to sell soon."

Follow-up email scripts

Email works best as the longer, more useful companion to your texts: listings, market notes and answers that don't fit in a text.

Subject: Homes like 42 Oak St
"Hi Mark, here are three homes similar to 42 Oak St in your price range, including one that isn't on the big portals yet. If any catch your eye, reply with the address and I'll set up a viewing. And if your plans have changed, just let me know and I'll adjust what I send. Jane"

Subject: What 210 Birch St might sell for
"Hi Linda, I pulled the recent sales on your street. Homes like yours have sold in a range of [range] over the past few months, depending mostly on updates and yard size. If you'd like a more exact number, I can walk through the house in 20 minutes. No obligation either way. Jane"

Keep emails skimmable, sign them with your name and include a clear opt-out line at the bottom.

How often should a real estate agent follow up? A 30-day plan

Front-load the first week, then slow down. Here's a simple plan that mixes text, email and calls:

DayChannelMessage
0 (minutes)TextFirst text with one question
0 (same day)CallQuick call; if no answer, no voicemail pitch, just "texted you"
1EmailThree similar homes or a price range
2–3TextUseful nudge (price history, showing activity)
7Call + text"Still looking, or should I pause?"
14EmailShort market update for their area
30TextCheck-in with one new listing or sale

After day 30, move the lead to a monthly rhythm: new listings that match, a sold-price note for their street, a seasonal tip. Low pressure, always useful.

What do you send a lead who went quiet?

Give them an easy way to answer, including "no". A polite break-up text often gets more replies than any other message:

The "should I pause?" text
"Hi Mark, I don't want to fill your phone if your plans have changed. Should I keep sending homes, pause for a few months, or stop? Any answer is totally fine."

Replies to this tell you exactly where each lead stands, which keeps your pipeline honest.
