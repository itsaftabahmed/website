How insurance agents can follow up on quote requests faster

How do insurance agents follow up on quote requests faster? Reply automatically within a minute by text and email, ask the two or three questions you need to start a quote, assign the lead to an agent with an alert, and keep a short follow-up sequence running until the shopper talks to someone. Speed matters more in insurance than almost anywhere, because shoppers usually request quotes from several agencies at once.

By Aftab Ahmed · November 9, 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways
  • Insurance shoppers often contact several agencies, so the first useful reply has a real advantage.
  • An automatic text and email within a minute buys time until a licensed agent can call.
  • Ask only what you need to start the quote (coverage type, ZIP code, renewal date) and leave the rest for the call.
  • Route each lead to one named agent with an alert, so no request sits in a shared inbox.
  • Follow up for about 10 days, then switch to a reminder before the shopper's renewal date.

Picture a homeowner who just got a renewal notice with a 20% increase. On a Tuesday evening they fill in quote forms on three agency websites. The first agency texts back in a minute and books a call for the next morning. The other two call the next afternoon, and the homeowner has already moved on. That is how most quote requests are won or lost, and it has little to do with price.

Why is speed so important for insurance leads?

Because shoppers compare. Someone requesting a quote online is usually also asking other agencies or comparison sites, and the agency that starts the conversation first sets the pace. A slow reply doesn't just delay the sale; it often hands it to someone else. The general research behind this is in my speed to lead guide.

Bought leads make speed even more important, because the same lead may have gone to several agents at once.

What should the first reply say?

Confirm the request, name a real person, and give one easy next step. For example:

Example first text: "Hi Chris, this is Dana at Lakeside Insurance. Thanks for your home insurance quote request. To get started, when does your current policy renew? You can also pick a time to talk here: [link]. Reply STOP to opt out."

Send an email at the same time with the same booking link and a short note on what happens next. Keep both free of coverage advice; that belongs in the conversation with a licensed agent.

Which details should you collect first?

Only what you need to start quoting or to route the lead. Long forms lower the number of people who finish them. A good starting set:

  • Line of coverage: auto, home, renters, life, business, or a bundle.
  • ZIP code, which tells you whether you can write the policy and who should handle it.
  • Current renewal date, the single best signal of how urgent the lead is.
  • Best time to call, so the agent calls when the shopper can talk.

Vehicle details, property details and driver history can come later, through a secure form or on the call. Avoid collecting sensitive personal information such as Social Security numbers by text.

How should leads be assigned to agents?

Every lead should have one owner within minutes. Simple rules work well:

RuleExample
By line of businessCommercial requests go to the commercial agent; personal lines go to the personal lines team
By territoryZIP codes in your second office's area go to that office
Round robinPersonal auto leads rotate between three agents
EscalationIf nobody opens the lead in 15 minutes, the agency owner gets an alert

The escalation rule is what stops leads going cold on busy days. When I set this up for agencies, it is usually the piece that makes the biggest difference, because it turns "someone will get to it" into a named person with a deadline.

What does a good follow-up sequence look like?

About 10 days of mixed texts, emails and calls, stopping as soon as the shopper replies or books:

  1. Minute 0: automatic text and email (above), plus an alert to the assigned agent.
  2. Within 15 minutes: first call from the agent during business hours.
  3. Day 1: second call at a different time; text if no answer: "Tried you earlier about your home quote. Is tomorrow morning better?"
  4. Day 3: email explaining what you need for the quote and how long it takes.
  5. Day 6: short text with a yes or no question.
  6. Day 10: final text: "Should I close your quote request, or check back closer to your renewal date?"

What about shoppers who aren't ready yet?

Many shoppers are early: their renewal is months away. Don't keep messaging them weekly. Save the renewal date and schedule one reminder about 30 to 45 days before it, with a short note offering to review their options. This is where the renewal date question pays off.

The same scheduling helps existing clients too. A review reminder before each renewal gives agents a reason to call, and it lets you mention other coverage the client may need. My lead follow-up and AI assistant setup for insurance agencies covers both new quotes and renewals.

What should an agency track?

Four numbers tell you whether the system works, and most CRMs can report them:

  • Time to first contact: minutes from request to first real conversation, not just the automatic text.
  • Contact rate: the share of leads an agent actually spoke to.
  • Quote rate: the share of contacted leads that received a quote.
  • Bound policies by lead source: which websites, ads or lead vendors produce clients, not just leads.

Review them monthly. If the contact rate is low, the problem is follow-up. If contact is good but few leads get quoted, look at lead quality or the questions on your form. Comparing bound policies by source is also the clearest way to decide whether a lead vendor is worth renewing.

What rules should agencies keep in mind?

A few basics, as general information rather than legal advice:

  • Consent for texts and automated calls: collect clear consent on your quote forms and honor opt-outs right away. Be careful with purchased leads; confirm how consent was collected.
  • Licensed advice: automated messages and assistants should handle scheduling and basic intake. Coverage recommendations and quotes come from licensed producers, as state licensing rules require; the NAIC has an overview of producer licensing.
  • Email: include your business address and an unsubscribe link, as the FTC's CAN-SPAM guide explains.
  • Carrier and state advertising rules: check your carrier agreements and state rules before using carrier names or prices in messages.

FAQ

How fast should an insurance agent respond to a quote request?

As fast as possible. An automatic text and email within a minute, and a call from an agent within minutes during business hours, gives you the best chance of starting the conversation before another agency does.

How many times should you follow up with an insurance lead?

About 6 calls, texts and emails over 10 days usually covers it, stopping as soon as the shopper replies. After that, schedule a reminder before their renewal date instead of messaging weekly.

Can an AI assistant follow up with insurance leads?

Yes, for administrative steps: confirming the request, collecting basic details and booking a call. Coverage advice and quotes should come from a licensed agent, so set the assistant to hand those conversations over.
