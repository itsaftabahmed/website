How to measure your lead response time (and what a good one is)

Lead response time is the time between a new inquiry arriving (a form, call, text or ad lead) and your first real reply. To measure it, take your last 20 to 30 leads, subtract each arrival time from the first reply time, and look at two numbers: the median reply time and the share of leads answered within 5 minutes. A good lead response time for a small business is under 5 minutes during business hours and an automatic reply within a minute at any hour.

By Aftab Ahmed · November 23, 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways
  • Measure the gap between when a lead arrives and the first real reply, for every lead source.
  • Use the median and the share answered within 5 minutes; the average hides slow replies.
  • Check evenings and weekends separately, because that is where most businesses lose speed.
  • A good target is an automatic reply within a minute and a person within 5 minutes during business hours.
  • Re-measure every month so you can see whether changes actually worked.

Most business owners believe they reply fast. The numbers often say otherwise, especially for the lead that came in at 6:40 pm on a Friday. Measuring lead response time takes about 30 minutes with a spreadsheet, and it tells you exactly where customers are slipping away.

What is lead response time?

Lead response time is the time between a new lead reaching your business and the first real reply that person gets. A "real reply" means a call, text or email that answers them or offers a next step. An automatic "we got your message" email with nothing else does not count as a reply, while an automatic text that offers two appointment times does.

It is the number behind speed to lead: the faster your first reply, the more of your leads you reach while they are still deciding.

Where do you find the timestamps?

Every lead source records when the lead arrived. You need that time and the time of your first reply:

Lead sourceArrival timeFirst reply time
Website formForm notification email or CRM "created" dateYour first sent email or text, or the call log
Phone call you missedMissed call in your phone system's call logYour callback or text in the call log
Facebook or Instagram lead formLead created time in Meta's leads center or your CRMFirst text, call or email to that lead
Google Business Profile messages or callsMessage time in the profile inbox or call historyYour reply time in the same inbox

If your leads already go into a CRM, it usually shows both times on each contact. If they live in email inboxes and a phone, a simple spreadsheet works: one row per lead with source, arrival time and first reply time.

How do you calculate lead response time?

  1. Take your last 20 to 30 leads, or the last month if you get fewer.
  2. For each lead, subtract the arrival time from the first reply time. Leads that never got a reply count as "no reply", not as zero.
  3. Sort the reply times from fastest to slowest and take the middle one. That is your median.
  4. Count how many were answered within 5 minutes and divide by the total. That is your share under 5 minutes.
  5. Count how many got no reply at all.

A hypothetical example with 10 leads: replies took 2, 3, 4, 12, 25, 40, 90 minutes, then 5 hours and 18 hours, and one lead never got a reply. The average of the nine replies is over 2.5 hours, which sounds terrible. The median is 25 minutes, which sounds fine. The share under 5 minutes is 3 out of 10, or 30%, and 1 lead in 10 was never answered. Those last two numbers are the ones that tell you what to fix.

Why not the average? One lead answered the next morning can triple your average while most leads were answered quickly. The median shows a typical lead's experience, and the share under 5 minutes shows how many leads you reach while they are still interested.

What is a good lead response time?

  • Under 1 minute: realistic only with an automatic reply, such as a text that answers and offers times. This is the right target for every lead, day or night.
  • Under 5 minutes: a strong result for a person replying during business hours.
  • 30 minutes to a few hours: common, and where many leads have already contacted another business.
  • Next day: you are mostly reaching people who have booked elsewhere.

Research has pointed this way for years. A widely cited Harvard Business Review study of online leads found that many companies took hours or longer to reply, and that the odds of reaching a lead dropped quickly after the first hour. Your own numbers matter more than any benchmark, so measure, change one thing, and measure again.

Which leads should you check separately?

One overall number hides the problem. Split your list three ways:

  • By time: business hours, evenings and weekends. After-hours leads are usually the slowest.
  • By source: calls, website forms, ad leads and messages. Ad leads are often the slowest because they arrive in a separate inbox nobody watches.
  • By person: if several people reply to leads, check each one.

The slowest group is where to start. For many service businesses that is missed calls after hours, which missed call text back fixes in a day.

FAQ

How do you calculate average lead response time?

Subtract each lead's arrival time from the time of the first reply, add those times together and divide by the number of leads. Also work out the median and the share of leads answered within 5 minutes, because a few very slow replies can make the average misleading.

What is a good lead response time for a small business?

A good lead response time is an automatic reply within a minute and a reply from a person within 5 minutes during business hours. Many small businesses take hours, so getting under 5 minutes puts you ahead of most local competitors.

Does an automatic email count as a response?

Only if it moves the lead forward. A generic "we received your message" email does not count; an automatic text or email that answers the question or offers appointment times does.
