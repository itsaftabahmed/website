Law firm client intake: how to stop losing potential cases

A potential client is hurt in a car accident on Friday afternoon. By Saturday morning they have called three firms. One goes to voicemail, one has a receptionist who takes a message, and one answers, asks a few clear questions and books a consultation for Monday. That third firm usually gets the case. Most firms do not lose cases because of their lawyering. They lose them at intake, before an attorney ever hears about them.

Law firm client intake is the process from first contact to signed engagement: answering the inquiry, screening the matter, checking conflicts, booking the consultation and following up. A good process does three things well. It responds fast, it qualifies consistently, and it makes the next step obvious to someone who is often stressed or scared.

Here is how to tighten each part, with notes on where ethics rules shape what you can do.

Where do law firms lose potential cases?

Usually in the gaps between steps. The most common leaks are easy to spot once you look for them:

  • Unanswered calls during court, lunch, evenings and weekends.
  • Web forms that sit until someone checks the inbox the next business day.
  • Inconsistent screening, where each staff member asks different questions and good matters get turned away.
  • No follow-up after a consultation, so undecided clients drift to another firm.
  • Declined matters with no referral, which wastes goodwill that could come back as future work.

Each of these is a process problem, which means each can be fixed with a clear routine and the right tools.

How fast should a firm respond to new inquiries?

As fast as you can, ideally within minutes. Someone with an urgent legal problem rarely waits a day for a call back. In practice areas like personal injury, criminal defense and family law, the first firm to respond with a clear next step often wins the client.

That doesn't mean an attorney has to answer every call. It means every inquiry gets an immediate, useful response: a person, an answering service with your script, or an automated reply that acknowledges the inquiry, sets expectations and offers a time to talk. The speed to lead guide explains why those first minutes matter so much, and missed call text back covers calls that ring out.

After hours is where many firms lose the most. Inquiries often come in evenings and weekends, when people finally have time to deal with a legal problem. Decide what happens to those inquiries before they arrive.

What should an intake script include?

A consistent intake script makes screening faster and fairer, and it protects the firm. A practical structure:

  1. Greeting and expectations. Who they are speaking with, and that this is an initial screening, not legal advice.
  2. Contact details, including the best way and time to reach them.
  3. Names for a conflict check: the potential client and the other parties involved, before detailed facts.
  4. Matter type and key screening questions for your practice areas (for example, date of incident, location, whether they already have a lawyer).
  5. Urgency: deadlines, court dates or statute of limitations concerns that need an attorney's attention quickly.
  6. Next step: book a consultation, send documents through a secure link, or explain why the firm can't help and offer a referral.

Write the screening questions for each practice area with your attorneys, so staff (or an assistant) can qualify matters without making legal judgments.

How do you stay within ethics rules?

Keep intake to gathering information and scheduling, and leave advice to attorneys. A few principles most firms follow (your state bar's rules are the final word):

  • Make clear that contacting the firm does not create an attorney-client relationship until an engagement is signed
  • Run a conflict check before collecting detailed, sensitive facts
  • Never let staff or automated tools give legal advice or predict outcomes
  • Keep confidential details out of plain text messages and use secure forms or portals
  • Follow your state's rules on advertising, solicitation and follow-up contact with prospective clients

Prospective clients can be owed confidentiality even if you never take the case, which is another reason to collect only what you need at first contact.

What parts of intake can be automated?

The repetitive parts, while people keep the judgment. An AI intake assistant and workflow automation can:

  • Answer calls, texts and website chats around the clock with your approved script.
  • Ask the screening questions for each practice area and capture the answers in your case management system or CRM.
  • Flag urgent matters and alert the on-call attorney or intake lead.
  • Offer consultation times from your calendar and send confirmations and reminders.
  • Follow up with prospects who haven't booked or signed, with messages your attorneys approve.

It should not decide whether the firm takes a case, give legal opinions or discuss fees beyond what you have approved. Those stay with your team.

FAQ

What is client intake at a law firm?

Client intake is the process from a potential client's first contact to a signed engagement. It covers answering the inquiry, screening the matter, checking conflicts, booking a consultation and following up until the person hires the firm or is referred elsewhere.

Can AI handle legal intake?

AI can handle the routine parts: answering inquiries around the clock, asking approved screening questions, booking consultations and sending reminders. It should not give legal advice or decide whether to take a case, and confidential details should go through secure channels.

When should a conflict check happen during intake?

As early as possible, ideally after collecting names of the parties involved and before the potential client shares detailed facts. This protects the firm from receiving information that could create a conflict with an existing client.
