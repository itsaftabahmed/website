AI receptionist pricing models compared: per minute vs flat rate vs per call

Which AI receptionist pricing model is cheapest? Per-minute billing usually wins at low call volume, a flat rate wins once you pass a few hundred minutes a month, and per-call billing only makes sense when your calls run long. The exact crossover depends on two numbers you can pull from your phone bill: minutes per month and average call length.

By Aftab Ahmed · November 16, 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways
  • Per-minute billing is usually cheapest for low volume or short calls.
  • A flat rate becomes cheaper once your monthly minutes pass the flat price divided by the per-minute rate.
  • Per-call billing beats per-minute billing only when the average call is longer than the per-call rate divided by the per-minute rate.
  • Bundles with overage look cheap but can cost the most in a busy month.
  • Billing increments, spam calls and overage rates change the real price more than the headline number.

Two plans can quote you the same "starting at" price and end up hundreds of dollars apart by the end of a busy month. The difference is the pricing model, not the plan name. This guide shows where each model wins, with break-even math you can redo with your own numbers in a few minutes. For typical price ranges, see my guide to how much an AI receptionist costs.

What are the four AI receptionist pricing models?

Almost every AI receptionist plan is one of these four, or a mix of them:

  • Per minute: you pay for every minute the assistant spends on calls.
  • Flat rate: one monthly price, often with a fair-use limit in the terms.
  • Per call: a fixed price for each answered call, however long it lasts.
  • Bundle plus overage: a monthly price that includes a set number of minutes or calls, then a higher rate after that.

Where does each model become the cheapest?

Each model has a break-even point, and you can work it out with simple division. The rates below are example values chosen to show the math, not real vendor prices:

ComparisonExample ratesBreak-even
Flat rate vs per minute$150 a month vs $0.40 a minute$150 ÷ $0.40 = 375 minutes a month
Per call vs per minute$1.50 a call vs $0.40 a minute$1.50 ÷ $0.40 = calls longer than 3.75 minutes
Bundle vs flat rate$99 for 200 minutes, then $0.50 vs $150 flat200 + ($150 − $99) ÷ $0.50 = 302 minutes a month

Put together with these example rates: below about 250 minutes a month, per minute is cheapest. Between about 250 and 300 minutes, the bundle wins. Above about 300 minutes, the flat rate wins and keeps getting better value the busier you are. Per call only wins if your calls average close to 4 minutes or more.

Tip: Work out the break-even for your busiest month, not your average one. A plan that is cheapest in March can be the most expensive in July for a cooling company.

Why does call length matter so much?

Because it decides whether you should pay by the minute or by the call. Short, simple calls ("are you open Saturday?", "can I book Tuesday?") are cheap on per-minute plans. Long calls, such as a law firm intake or an insurance quote conversation, can cost more per minute than a per-call plan would charge.

Your phone system's call log shows the average length. If you can't find it, time ten normal calls. Home service businesses often see short, urgent calls, while law firms and insurance agencies tend to see longer ones. Those are patterns, not rules, so check your own numbers.

A quick example with the same hypothetical rates: 200 calls a month at 1.5 minutes is 300 minutes, so per minute costs $120 and per call costs $300. The same 200 calls at 5 minutes is 1,000 minutes, so per minute costs $400 and per call still costs $300. Same number of calls, opposite answer.

Which billing terms change the real cost?

The model is only half of the price. These terms can raise the bill without changing the headline number:

  • Billing increments: if every call is rounded up to the next full minute, a 2-minute-10-second call is billed as 3 minutes. That is nearly 40% more than the time actually used.
  • Spam and wrong numbers: ask whether screened robocalls and hang-ups count toward your minutes or calls.
  • Overage rate: the price per minute after a bundle runs out is usually higher than the price inside it.
  • Fair-use limits: some "unlimited" flat plans have a cap in the terms of service.
  • Minimum term and annual prepay: a cheaper monthly rate that locks you in for a year is not always cheaper.
  • Extras billed separately: text messages, extra numbers, call transfers and calendar or CRM connections.

Healthcare practices have one more check. If the assistant handles patient information, the vendor needs to sign a business associate agreement, which HHS describes here. Some vendors only include it on higher-priced plans, which can change which model is cheapest for you.

How do you compare two quotes fairly?

Convert every quote into one number: your total monthly cost at your real volume.

  1. Write down your monthly calls and average call length, for a normal month and your busiest month.
  2. For each quote, apply its model and billing increment to those numbers.
  3. Add the monthly extras and spread any setup fee over 12 months.
  4. Compare the busiest-month totals side by side.

Ask each vendor for these terms in writing before you sign. If a vendor can't tell you how a 2-minute-10-second call is billed, treat that as an answer.

Which model should a small business pick?

A practical rule of thumb from setting these up for service businesses:

  • Only covering after-hours or overflow calls? Start per minute. Volume is low and unpredictable.
  • Answering every call, with steady volume? A flat rate is usually simplest and cheapest, as long as the fair-use limit is well above your busiest month.
  • Few calls, but long ones? Price per call against per minute using the break-even above.
  • Considering a bundle? Only if your busiest month stays inside it. Otherwise the overage erases the saving.

Whichever model you choose, the assistant should say it is an AI when asked, and any automated follow-up calls or texts need the customer's consent. The FCC has confirmed that AI-generated voices in robocalls fall under existing calling rules.

FAQ

Is per-minute or flat-rate pricing better for an AI receptionist?

Per-minute pricing is usually cheaper at low volume and flat-rate pricing at higher volume. Divide the flat monthly price by the per-minute rate: if your monthly minutes are above that number, the flat rate is cheaper.

When does per-call pricing make sense?

When your calls are long. Divide the per-call price by the per-minute rate to get a call length; if your average call is longer than that, per-call billing costs less than per-minute billing.

What hidden costs should I check in an AI receptionist contract?

Check how calls are rounded (by the second, 30 seconds or full minute), whether spam calls count, the overage rate, fair-use limits on unlimited plans, minimum terms, and extras such as texts, numbers and integrations.
