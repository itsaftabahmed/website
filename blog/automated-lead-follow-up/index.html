How to follow up with leads automatically (SMS, email, calls)

How do you follow up with leads automatically? Connect every lead source to one CRM, send a text and email within a minute, alert a person to call, then let a short sequence of texts and emails run for about two weeks until the lead replies or books. Automation handles the timing and the reminders; your team handles the conversations.

By Aftab Ahmed · November 5, 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways
  • Automated follow-up means every new lead gets a fast first reply and a planned series of texts, emails and call tasks, without anyone remembering to send them.
  • The first reply matters most: a text and email within a minute, plus an alert for someone to call.
  • A simple 14-day sequence of about 6 to 8 touches covers most service businesses; stop it the moment the lead replies or books.
  • Texting leads needs consent, an easy opt-out and a registered business number; email needs a real sender and an unsubscribe option.
  • Automation should start conversations, not replace them. Hand replies to a person quickly.

Most service businesses don't lose leads because their ads are bad. They lose them because nobody follows up after the first missed call or the first unanswered text. Busy people often miss the first message, and many businesses stop after one or two tries. Automation fixes the part people forget: the second, third and fourth touch.

Here is the setup I build for service businesses, using a home services company as the example. The same structure works for dental offices, law firms, med spas and real estate agents.

What is automated lead follow-up?

It is a set of rules in your CRM that react when a new lead arrives. The system sends messages at planned times, creates call tasks for your team, and stops automatically when the lead replies, books or asks to stop. Nothing is sent at random, and nobody needs to keep a list of who to chase.

A typical setup has three parts:

  • One inbox for leads: web forms, Facebook and Google lead forms, missed calls and chat all land in the same CRM.
  • A sequence: the planned texts, emails and call tasks, with timing.
  • Stop rules: any reply, booking or opt-out ends the sequence immediately.

What should happen in the first five minutes?

A reply within a minute, by text and email, and a call from a person as soon as possible. This is where most of the value is. The speed to lead guide explains why the first few minutes decide who wins the job.

For a homeowner who requests an AC repair quote, the first text might read:

Example first text: "Hi Maria, this is Dan from Cool Air Heating & Cooling. Got your request about the AC. Is it blowing warm air or not turning on at all? I can have a tech out today or tomorrow. Reply STOP to opt out."

It names the business, refers to what they asked about, asks one easy question, and offers a next step. At the same moment, the office gets an alert to call. If a call comes in and nobody answers, a missed call text back does the same job.

What does a good 14-day sequence look like?

About 6 to 8 touches over two weeks, mixing channels, then a slower monthly check-in. Here is the version I start with:

WhenWhat happens
Minute 0Text and email confirming the request, with one question and a booking link
Minute 0 to 5Call task for the team; if no answer, short voicemail
Hour 3Text: "Still want us to take a look? Here are two open times."
Day 1Second call attempt, at a different time of day
Day 2Email with something useful: your service call fee, what to expect on the visit, a few reviews
Day 4Text with a simple yes or no question
Day 7Email or text checking whether the problem is solved
Day 14Final text: "Should I close your request, or keep it open?"
Monthly afterSeasonal tips or a reminder, until they opt out

That last day-14 message often gets more replies than the ones before it, because it is easy to answer. Adjust the timing for your industry: emergency services need a tighter first day, while law firms or real estate agents may stretch the sequence over several weeks. Text, email or calls: which works best?

All three, for different jobs. Texts get read quickly and suit short questions and booking links. Calls work best for the first contact and for leads who are clearly ready. Email suits longer information, such as pricing details, what to expect, and documents.

  • Text for speed and replies.
  • Calls for qualifying and booking. Keep these with your team, not robocalls.
  • Email for detail and longer-term nurture.

What rules apply to automated texts and emails?

A few basics keep you compliant and out of spam filters. This is general information, not legal advice:

  • Get consent for texts on your forms in clear words, and keep a record of it. Carriers expect consent before business texts; Twilio explains how to collect consent for A2P 10DLC texting.
  • Register your business texting number (A2P 10DLC registration in the US) through your texting provider, or messages may be filtered.
  • Make opting out easy: honor STOP replies right away and stop the whole sequence.
  • For email, use an accurate sender and subject line, include your business address and an unsubscribe link, as the FTC's CAN-SPAM compliance guide describes.
  • Send at reasonable hours in the lead's time zone, and only to people who contacted you.

What mistakes make automated follow-up backfire?

  • Messages that sound like a robot. Write the way your best office person talks.
  • No stop rules. Nothing annoys a customer more than getting "Still interested?" after they already booked.
  • Too many messages on day one. One text, one email and a call is enough.
  • Nobody watching replies. Every reply should notify a person and be answered within working hours.
  • Leads outside the CRM. If Facebook leads go to one inbox and web forms to another, some will be missed.

How do you set it up step by step?

  1. List every lead source: website form, phone, Google and Facebook lead forms, chat, referrals.
  2. Pick one CRM and connect each source to it, directly or through an automation tool.
  3. Add consent wording to every form that collects a phone number.
  4. Write the first text and email, then the rest of the sequence above, in your own voice.
  5. Set stop rules for replies, bookings and opt-outs, and alerts for your team.
  6. Test it with your own phone from every lead source before turning it on.
  7. Review after 30 days: how many leads were reached, how many booked, and which message got the most replies.

FAQ

How many times should you follow up with a lead?

For most service businesses, about 6 to 8 touches over the first two weeks, mixing texts, emails and calls, then a monthly check-in. Stop as soon as the lead replies, books or opts out.

Is it legal to send automated texts to leads?

Generally yes, when the person gave consent, for example by submitting a form with clear texting consent wording, and you include an easy way to opt out. Rules vary by situation and state, so check yours, and register your business texting number with your provider.

What software do I need for automated follow-up?

A CRM that can send texts and emails on a schedule and create tasks for your team. Many small businesses use an all-in-one CRM, or connect their existing tools with an automation platform. The setup matters more than the brand.
