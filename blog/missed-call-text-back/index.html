Missed call text back: never lose a caller again

What happens when someone calls your business and nobody picks up? For most small businesses, the honest answer is nothing: the caller hangs up and tries the next name on the list. Missed call text back changes that. Within seconds of an unanswered call, the caller gets a friendly text from your business number, and the conversation keeps going instead of ending.

By Aftab Ahmed · October 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways
  • Missed call text back automatically texts anyone whose call you don't answer, usually within a few seconds.
  • It works because callers who hang up are still looking for help, and a text lets them reply without calling again.
  • The first text should be short, name your business, and ask one easy question about what they need.
  • In the US, business texting usually requires A2P 10DLC registration, and every text flow needs a clear way to opt out.
  • It can normally run on the number customers already call, through your phone provider or a business texting tool.

Missed call text back is a simple automation: when a call to your business goes unanswered, your phone system sends the caller a text message from the same number, usually within a few seconds. The text apologizes for missing them, says who you are, and asks what they need. The caller can then reply by text whenever suits them, and you (or an assistant) pick it up from there.

It's one of the cheapest fixes in lead generation, because it saves leads you already paid to get. No new ads, no new staff, just a faster reply to people who already wanted to talk to you.

What is missed call text back?

It's an automatic text reply to unanswered calls. Your phone line or texting tool watches for calls that ring out, go to voicemail or get declined, and sends a pre-written message to the caller's mobile number.

Most setups also handle a few details for you:

  • Business hours. A different message after hours ("We're closed for the night, reply here and we'll get back to you first thing").
  • Known numbers. Suppliers, staff and existing contacts can be skipped so they don't get a sales-style text.
  • Repeat callers. Someone who calls three times in five minutes gets one text, not three.
  • Landlines. Texts to landline numbers fail, so good tools detect them and alert you to call back instead.

Why does it work so well?

Because the caller's problem didn't go away when you missed the call. They still need a plumber, a dentist or a lawyer, and they're usually about to call someone else. A text that arrives within seconds catches them before they do.

Texting also suits how people behave. Many people would rather not leave a voicemail, but will happily reply to a text, especially if they're at work or somewhere they can't talk. And a text thread is easy to come back to later, while a voicemail usually means waiting for a call back that might come at a bad time.

There's a cost angle too. If you run Google Ads or Facebook ads, every missed call is a lead you've already paid for. The speed to lead guide covers why replying in minutes matters so much, and missed call text back is the fastest way to get there for phone leads.

Track how many conversations start from missed-call texts for the first month. It's often the clearest number for showing what the automation is worth, because every one of those would otherwise have been a silent hang-up.

What should the first text say?

Keep it short, human and ending in one easy question. The goal is a reply, not a sales pitch. Here are examples you can adapt (the business names are made up):

Business typeExample first text
Home services"Hi, this is Northside Plumbing. Sorry we missed your call, we're on a job. What's going on, and what's your zip code?"
Dental or medical"Hi, it's Bright Smile Dental. Sorry we missed you. Are you looking to book an appointment, or is it about something else?"
Law firm"Hi, this is Carter Law. Sorry we couldn't answer. What kind of legal matter can we help with? Please don't share sensitive details by text."
Real estate"Hi, it's Jenna with Oak Realty. Sorry I missed your call, I'm at a showing. Are you looking to buy, sell or both?"

A few things to avoid: links in the first text (they look like spam and can hurt delivery), long paragraphs, and anything that sounds automated, like "Your call is important to us." For healthcare and legal businesses, keep personal or medical details out of the text thread and move those conversations to a secure channel.

What US texting rules apply?

Two things matter most: registering your number for business texting, and giving people a way to opt out. Neither is complicated, but skipping them can get your messages blocked.

  • A2P 10DLC registration. US carriers expect business texts from a regular local number to be registered through your texting provider. Unregistered numbers can see messages filtered or blocked.
  • Opt-out handling. Anyone who replies STOP should stop receiving texts automatically, and your tool should handle that for you.
  • Stay conversational. Replying to someone who just called you about their request is generally treated as a normal conversational message. Sending them marketing texts later is different and needs proper consent.
  • Sensible hours. Automated follow-ups beyond the first reply should respect normal hours in the caller's time zone.

Rules differ by state and change over time, so check with your texting provider, and get legal advice if you plan to send marketing messages.

How do you set it up on your current number?

In most cases you keep the number customers already know. There are three common routes:

  1. Your phone provider. Many business VoIP providers include missed-call text back or offer it as an add-on. Check this first, because it's often already paid for.
  2. A CRM or lead platform. Many CRMs built for service businesses include it, and connect the text thread to the rest of your follow-up.
  3. Call forwarding to a texting-enabled number. If your current provider can't text, unanswered calls can forward to a number that can, while callers still dial your main number.

Setup usually takes a day or two of work plus the time for A2P registration to be approved. Test it from a few phones (iPhone, Android, a landline) before switching it on for everyone.

What happens after they reply?

This is where most of the value is, and where many setups stop too early. A reply needs a fast answer, or you've just moved the missed call into a missed text.

You have two options. A person on your team can get an instant alert and reply from a shared inbox or app. Or an AI assistant that answers and books appointments can carry the conversation: ask a couple of qualifying questions, share your hours or starting prices, and offer real times from your calendar. Urgent or complex conversations still get handed to a person.

FAQ

Does missed call text back work with landlines?

It can watch calls to a landline business number, but the text has to come from a texting-enabled number, and it can only be delivered to callers using mobile phones. Good tools detect landline callers and alert you to call them back instead.

Will it annoy people who called by mistake?

Rarely, if the text is short and polite. People who called by mistake usually ignore it or reply that it was an error. To avoid texting staff, suppliers and existing contacts, most tools let you exclude known numbers.

How much does missed call text back cost?

Costs vary by provider. It is often included with business phone or CRM plans, or available as a small monthly add-on plus per-message fees. Registration for business texting may also carry a one-time fee.
