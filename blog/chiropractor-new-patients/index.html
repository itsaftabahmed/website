How chiropractors can get more new patients without discounts

The most common mistake I see in chiropractic marketing is leading with a cheap first visit. A $29 exam fills the schedule for a week, but it tends to attract people shopping on price, and many of them never come back for the care plan. You can grow new patients without discounting by fixing what happens when someone in pain is searching right now: how fast you answer, what they see on Google, and whether your happy patients send others your way.

By Aftab Ahmed · October 19, 2026 · 7 min read

Key takeaways
  • Discount offers bring volume, but often attract price shoppers who drop off before finishing a care plan.
  • People in pain usually book with the first clinic that answers, so reply speed is one of the cheapest growth levers.
  • A strong Google Business Profile with recent reviews does much of the convincing before a patient ever calls.
  • High-intent Google searches like "chiropractor near me" are usually a better paid channel than broad social offers.
  • A simple referral and reactivation routine grows new patients from people who already trust you.

The short version: compete on access and trust instead of price. Be the clinic that answers within minutes, looks credible on Google, and makes it easy for current patients to recommend you. Those three things bring in patients who already value care, which is exactly who finishes a treatment plan.

None of this needs a bigger marketing budget. Most of it is setup work that keeps paying back once it runs on its own.

Why do discount offers backfire for chiropractors?

Because the offer shapes who shows up. A deep discount on the first visit tells people the visit is the product, so many treat it as a one-off. Clinics running heavy specials often report a busy calendar of first visits and a thin calendar of follow-ups.

There's a positioning cost too. Once your ads say $29, a full-price visit feels expensive to the same audience. And if you see Medicare or insurance patients, free or heavily discounted services can raise compliance questions, so check with your advisor before running them.

That doesn't mean never making an offer. A clear, valuable first visit ("full assessment and a plan you can understand, same-week appointments") is an offer too. It just sells certainty and speed rather than a price cut.

How much does reply speed matter?

A lot, because pain makes people impatient. Someone who threw out their back this morning will call two or three clinics and book with whoever answers first. If your front desk is checking in a patient and the call rolls to voicemail, that new patient is usually gone.

The fix is a safety net behind the phone:

  • Missed call text back, so every unanswered call gets a text within seconds
  • An instant reply to website forms and ad leads, day or night
  • Online booking for new-patient slots, so people can book without waiting for a call back
  • Same-day or next-day new-patient slots held open each week

An AI assistant connected to your schedule can answer common questions about the first visit, insurance and hours, and book the appointment while your team focuses on the patients in the room. The speed to lead guide explains why those first few minutes decide so much.

Try this: call your own clinic at 5:30 pm on a weekday from a number the front desk won't recognize. What a new patient experiences in that moment is your real marketing.

What should your Google profile show?

Enough to make the decision for them. For most clinics, the Google Business Profile is seen more often than the website. Make sure it has:

  • Accurate hours and categories, including "Chiropractor" as the primary category.
  • Recent reviews, ideally mentioning the problem that was solved (back pain, headaches, sports injury).
  • Real photos of the clinic, team and treatment rooms, not stock images.
  • A booking link that goes straight to new-patient times.
  • Answers to common questions in the Q&A and on your website: first visit length, what to wear, insurance.

Reviews come from asking consistently. An automatic text after the second or third visit, when patients are feeling better, usually works better than asking at the first appointment.

Which ads work best for chiropractors?

Ads that catch people who are already looking. Google searches like "chiropractor near me", "lower back pain chiropractor" or "sciatica treatment" come from people with a problem today. Those clicks cost more than a social media impression, but the intent is far higher.

A few principles for Google Ads for local clinics:

  1. Target a realistic radius, the area patients will actually drive from.
  2. Send clicks to a page about the specific problem, with booking right there.
  3. Use call assets so people can call straight from the ad, and make sure missed calls get a text back.
  4. Judge results by booked first visits and patients who start a care plan, not clicks.

Facebook and Instagram can still work, especially for awareness and retargeting people who visited your site. Lead with education and trust (a short video from the doctor on a common problem) rather than a discount.

How do you get more referrals?

Make referring easy and ask at the right moment. Happy patients are usually willing; they just don't think of it. Simple ways to build it into your routine:

  • A short text after a patient reports feeling better, with a link they can forward to a friend or family member.
  • Relationships with local gyms, running clubs, physical trainers and massage therapists who see people with the same problems.
  • Helpful talks or screenings at local employers where people sit at desks all day.

Keep referral rewards modest and compliant with your state rules. Often a sincere thank-you note works as well as any incentive.

Can past patients become new bookings?

Yes, and they're often the easiest bookings you'll get. Many patients stop coming when they feel better, then their problem returns months later. A gentle check-in ("It's been a while since your last visit, how's your back holding up?") brings a surprising number back.

Set this up once and let it run: a message to patients who haven't visited in 90 days, another at six months, each with a booking link. Pair it with care-plan reminders for current patients so fewer people drop off in the first place. FAQ

What is the best marketing for chiropractors?

The best channels are usually the ones that reach people with a problem today: Google search, a strong Google Business Profile with recent reviews, and referrals from happy patients. Fast replies to every call and message make all of them work better.

Are new patient specials worth it for chiropractors?

They can fill the calendar quickly, but deep discounts often attract price shoppers who don't continue care. A clear, valuable first visit offer focused on speed and a real assessment usually brings patients who stay. If you see Medicare or insurance patients, check compliance before offering free or discounted services.

How long does it take to grow new patients without discounts?

Reply speed and booking improvements can help within weeks, because they save leads you already get. Reviews, referrals and search visibility build over a few months and keep compounding after that.
