How much does an AI receptionist cost? Pricing explained for small businesses

How much does an AI receptionist cost? Self-serve AI receptionist software typically runs from about $30 to $300 a month, depending on how many calls or minutes you use. A done-for-you setup that is connected to your calendar, CRM and follow-up usually costs a few hundred dollars a month. The price on the plan page is only part of the bill, so the billing model matters as much as the number.

By Aftab Ahmed · November 12, 2026 · 8 min read

Key takeaways
  • Published self-serve AI receptionist plans commonly fall between about $30 and $300 a month; done-for-you setups cost more.
  • There are four billing models: flat monthly, per minute, per call, and included minutes with overage. The same call volume can cost very different amounts on each.
  • Count the extras before you compare: setup, phone numbers, texting, calendar or CRM connections and overage rates.
  • The useful comparison is not AI versus free. It is the monthly price against the calls you miss today and the staff time spent on the phone.
  • Estimate your monthly calls and average call length first. Those two numbers decide which plan is cheapest for you.

The honest answer is "it depends on your call volume", which is not much help when you are trying to set a budget. So this guide gives you the typical ranges, shows how each billing model behaves when the phone gets busy, and walks through one example you can copy with your own numbers.

How much does an AI receptionist cost per month?

Self-serve AI receptionist software typically costs somewhere between $30 and $300 a month. At the low end you get a basic assistant that answers, takes a message and sends you a summary. Toward the high end you get more minutes, appointment booking, texting and connections to your other tools.

A done-for-you setup costs more, usually a few hundred dollars a month, because someone builds the call flow, connects your calendar and CRM, tests it and keeps it working. For reference, my own plans start from $297 a month, and they cover the setup and the follow-up around the calls, not only the answering. Prices change often, so treat every range here as a starting point and check current plan pages before you decide.

If you are still deciding whether you need one at all, start with my guide to what an AI receptionist does for a small business.

What are the four pricing models?

AI receptionists are sold in four ways, and the model changes your bill more than the headline price does.

ModelHow you paySuits
Flat monthlyOne price, often with a fair-use limitSteady or high call volume; easy to budget
Per minuteA rate for every minute the assistant talksLow volume or short calls
Per callA rate for each answered call, whatever its lengthFew calls that run long
Included minutes plus overageA monthly bundle, then a higher rate after it runs outPredictable volume that stays inside the bundle

The last model is the most common and the easiest to misjudge. The bundle looks cheap, but a busy month can push you into overage, where each extra minute costs more than the minutes inside the plan.

What would 300 calls a month cost?

Here is a worked example. The business and the rates are hypothetical, chosen to show how the math works, so replace them with real quotes.

Say a plumbing company gets 300 calls a month to its main line and the average call lasts 2 minutes. That is 600 minutes.

  • Flat plan at $150 a month: $150, whether the month is quiet or busy.
  • Per-minute plan at $0.40 a minute: 600 x $0.40 = $240.
  • Per-call plan at $1.50 a call: 300 x $1.50 = $450.
  • Bundle of 200 minutes for $99, then $0.50 a minute: $99 + (400 x $0.50) = $299.

The plan with the lowest advertised price ($99) ends up costing about twice the flat plan. Now run the same numbers for 60 calls a month: 120 minutes costs $48 billed per minute, $90 billed per call, $99 on the bundle and still $150 flat. The cheapest plan flips. That is why I always ask for call volume before talking about price.

Tip: Spam and wrong-number calls count as minutes on many plans. Ask whether the assistant can screen them, and whether screened calls are billed.

What extra costs should you check?

The monthly fee is rarely the whole bill. Before you compare two quotes, ask each provider about these:

  • Setup or onboarding fee, and what it includes
  • Phone numbers: is one included, and what does each extra number cost?
  • Text messages, which are often billed separately from voice minutes
  • Calendar, CRM or booking-software connections (sometimes only on higher plans)
  • Overage rate once the included minutes or calls run out
  • Call transfers to your team, and whether transfer time is billed
  • Extra locations, languages or after-hours call flows
  • Contract length and how to cancel

Healthcare practices have one more check. If the assistant handles patient information, the vendor is acting as a business associate under HIPAA, and you need a signed agreement with them. HHS explains what a business associate agreement covers. Some providers only offer one on higher-priced plans, so ask before you sign up.

How does the cost compare with a receptionist or an answering service?

An AI receptionist costs far less than staff per hour covered, but it does a narrower job. The U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics lists the median pay for receptionists at $18.27 an hour, or $38,010 a year, as of May 2025. That is wages only, before payroll taxes and benefits, and it covers one person for about 40 hours a week.

A receptionist also does things software cannot: greeting people at the door, handling upset customers, noticing when something is wrong. So I don't treat this as a replacement. In most setups I build, the assistant takes the overflow, the lunch break and the after-hours calls, and the team keeps the conversations that need a person.

Live answering services sit in between. They are usually priced per minute or per call with a monthly minimum, and they tend to cost more than AI at the same volume because a person answers each call. The trade-offs are in my comparison of an AI receptionist vs an answering service.

Is an AI receptionist worth the money?

It is worth it when the calls it saves are worth more than the monthly fee, and for many service businesses that takes only one or two jobs a month. The math is simple:

  1. Count the calls you miss in a typical month (your phone system or carrier report shows this).
  2. Estimate how many of those were new customers.
  3. Multiply by your booking rate and your average job value.

A hypothetical example: 40 missed calls a month, half from new customers, a quarter of those would have booked, and an average job of $350. That is 5 jobs, or $1,750 a month, against a fee of a few hundred dollars. Your numbers will differ, and my missed call calculator does this sum for you.

It is not worth it if you miss very few calls, or if most callers need a detailed quote that only you can give. In that case a simple missed call text back is cheaper and may be enough.

How do you pick the right plan?

Work from your own call data, not the plan names.

  1. Pull last month's numbers. Total inbound calls, missed calls and average call length.
  2. Decide the job. After-hours only, overflow, or every call. This changes the minutes you need by a lot.
  3. Price it on each model. Use the method in the example above, for a normal month and for your busiest month.
  4. Add the extras. Setup, numbers, texting and connections from the checklist.
  5. Test before you commit. Call it yourself with your five most common questions. You can try one in my AI receptionist demo.

One rule applies to every plan: the assistant should say it is an AI when asked, and it should only follow up by automated call or text with people who agreed to that. The FCC has confirmed that AI-generated voices in robocalls fall under existing calling rules. Answering your own inbound calls is a different situation, but any outbound follow-up needs consent.

FAQ

How much does an AI receptionist cost per month?

Self-serve AI receptionist software typically costs about $30 to $300 a month, depending on call volume and features. Done-for-you setups that include calendar and CRM connections usually cost a few hundred dollars a month. Prices vary by provider and change often.

Is an AI receptionist cheaper than a human receptionist?

Per hour covered, yes. The BLS lists median receptionist pay at $18.27 an hour before taxes and benefits, while an AI receptionist covers nights and weekends for a monthly fee. It does a narrower job, so most businesses use it alongside staff, not instead of them.

Are there hidden fees with AI receptionists?

Often there are extras: setup fees, additional phone numbers, text messages, software connections and overage rates once included minutes run out. Ask for each of these in writing and price your busiest month before you choose a plan.
