Why do flooring companies lose good leads?

Because inquiries come in while the team is on a job site or in the showroom, the first conversation is spent gathering basics, and quotes go quiet with no planned follow-up.

Inquiries that wait

Calls and messages arrive while installers are on site, so homeowners hear from another company first.

Estimates without details

Visits are booked without knowing the rooms, square footage or material, so time is spent on jobs that don't fit.

Quotes that go quiet

Homeowners compare quotes, pick samples and talk it over, and without a follow-up plan many of them are never heard from again.

Ads that bring browsers

Ads get clicks and messages from people who are only looking at prices, and staff spend hours on them.

What does this look like for a new homeowner?

  1. A homeowner wants to replace the carpet in three bedrooms and messages your company from a Facebook ad on a Sunday evening.
  2. They get a reply within seconds asking which rooms, the rough size and whether they prefer hardwood, laminate or luxury vinyl.
  3. They answer and pick an in-home estimate time for Thursday from the booking link.
  4. The estimator gets the details ahead of the visit and brings the right samples, and the homeowner gets a reminder the day before.
  5. After the quote, follow-up messages answer questions and check in, and the job is booked the following week.
You stay in control: anything sensitive, complex or high-value is handed to you or your team, with the full conversation attached.

FAQ

Can the assistant give customers a price?

The assistant can share starting prices or ranges you approve, but final quotes come from your estimator after measuring, since room size, subfloor and materials change the price. That keeps the assistant from promising numbers you haven't signed off on.

Will customers know they are talking to an automated assistant?

Customers should know: the assistant says it is automated when asked. Most homeowners care more about a quick, helpful reply and an easy way to book than about who sends the first message. Anything unusual is passed to your team.

Does it work with the software we already use?

Often, yes. Many CRMs, calendars and field service tools can connect directly or through automation platforms. Where they can't, inquiries go to a simple CRM or shared calendar with alerts to your team, and automated texts are set up with clear consent and opt-out.
