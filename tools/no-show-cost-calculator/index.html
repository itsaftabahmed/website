How do you calculate the cost of no-shows?

The cost of no-shows is the number of missed appointments times what each visit is worth, minus the visits that still happen later because the person rebooked:

Monthly cost = appointments per week × 4.33 × no-show rate × average visit value × (1 − share that rebooks). Multiply by 12 for the yearly cost.

A worked example with hypothetical numbers: 100 appointments a week, a 12% no-show rate, a $200 average visit and 30% of no-shows rebooking later. That is about 52 missed appointments a month and 100 × 4.33 × 0.12 × $200 × 0.70 ≈ $7,270 lost a month, or about $87,300 a year. Cutting the rate from 12% to 6% would win back about half of that. These are example numbers; use your own.

How do you find your no-show rate?

Take the last 4 weeks in your scheduling or practice software. Count booked appointments and count no-shows (people who neither came nor cancelled). Divide no-shows by booked appointments and multiply by 100. Track late cancellations separately: you may refill those slots, while a no-show slot is usually lost. Check the rate by weekday, appointment type and how far ahead the visit was booked, because visits booked weeks in advance are often the ones people forget.

How can you reduce no-shows?

  • Confirm at booking: a text with the date, time, address and how to reschedule.
  • Remind before the visit: one reminder a day or two ahead, and one on the day for early appointments.
  • Make it two-way: let people reply to confirm or reschedule, so a "can't make it" becomes a rebooking instead of an empty slot.
  • Keep a waitlist: offer freed-up slots to people who want an earlier time.
  • Use deposits where they fit: for long or high-value appointments such as med spa treatments.

Practices should keep reminders free of sensitive details: date, time, location and how to confirm. The HHS HIPAA guidance for professionals covers what counts as protected health information. Automated texts need the person's consent and an easy way to opt out.

FAQ

How do you calculate the cost of no-shows?

Multiply your booked appointments per month by your no-show rate to get missed appointments, then multiply by the average value of a visit. Subtract the share of no-shows that rebook and still come in later, because that revenue is delayed rather than lost. Multiply by 12 for the yearly cost.

How do you calculate a no-show rate?

Divide the number of no-shows by the number of booked appointments in the same period and multiply by 100. Count every booked appointment, not only completed ones, and count late cancellations separately if you can refill those slots.

What is a good no-show rate?

There is no single benchmark, because no-show rates vary widely by industry, booking lead time and patient or customer type. The useful target is your own rate going down month after month; track it monthly and compare it after you add reminders, confirmations or deposits.

How can a business reduce no-shows?

The most practical steps are a confirmation at booking, reminders by text before the visit, a two-way reply so people can confirm or reschedule in one tap, a waitlist to refill empty slots, and deposits for long or high-value appointments where that fits your business.
