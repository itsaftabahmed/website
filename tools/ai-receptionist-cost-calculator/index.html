How do you calculate AI receptionist cost?

Start with minutes: calls per month × average call length × the share of calls the AI answers. Then price those minutes on each of the four billing models:

  • Flat monthly: the plan price, whatever the volume (many flat plans have a fair-use limit, so check it).
  • Per minute: minutes × the per-minute rate.
  • Per call: answered calls × the per-call rate.
  • Bundle plus overage: the bundle price, plus (minutes beyond the bundle × the overage rate).

Add monthly extras to each. A worked example with hypothetical rates: 300 calls at 2 minutes is 600 minutes. A $150 flat plan costs $150. At $0.40 a minute it costs $240. At $1.50 a call it costs $450. A $99 bundle with 200 minutes and $0.50 overage costs $99 + (400 × $0.50) = $299. The plan with the lowest advertised price is not the cheapest one here.

How does the cost change by industry?

Call length and call timing matter more than the industry name, but they follow patterns:

  • Home services: short, urgent calls, many after hours. Per-minute bills stay low per call but spike in peak season. See automation for home service businesses.
  • Dental and medical: steady daytime volume with scheduling questions. If the assistant handles patient information, the vendor needs to sign a business associate agreement; HHS explains what that covers. See automation for dental practices.
  • Law firms: fewer but longer intake calls, so per-minute and per-call plans behave very differently. See automation for law firms.
  • Med spas: booking and pricing questions, often in the evening. See automation for med spas.

FAQ

How much does an AI receptionist cost per month?

Self-serve plans commonly run from about $30 to $300 a month, and done-for-you setups usually cost a few hundred dollars a month. The real figure depends on how many minutes or calls you use, so enter your own volume in the calculator above.

How do I calculate my AI receptionist cost?

Multiply your monthly calls by your average call length to get minutes. Then price those minutes on each billing model: a flat fee, minutes times the per-minute rate, calls times the per-call rate, or a bundle price plus overage for minutes beyond the bundle. Add any monthly extras.

Is per-minute or flat-rate pricing cheaper for an AI receptionist?

It depends on volume. Per-minute billing is usually cheaper at low volume or with short calls, and a flat plan is usually cheaper once you pass a few hundred minutes a month. The calculator shows the point where they cross for your numbers.

What hidden fees should I add to the estimate?

Common extras are setup or onboarding, additional phone numbers, text messages, calendar or CRM connections, call transfers and overage rates. Ask each provider for these in writing and add the monthly ones to the extras field.

Are the rates in the calculator real vendor prices?

No. The starting rates are example values so the math is easy to follow. Replace them with the rates from the plans you are comparing.
