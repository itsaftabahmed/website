How do you calculate what speed to lead is worth?

Split your leads into two groups: the ones you reply to within 5 minutes and the ones who wait longer. Each group has its own close rate. The value of replying faster is the number of leads you move from the slow group to the fast group, times the difference in close rate, times what a customer is worth:

Extra customers = leads per month × (target fast share − current fast share) × (fast close rate − slow close rate), and extra revenue = extra customers × average job value.

A worked example with hypothetical numbers: 100 leads a month, 20% answered within 5 minutes today, a 30% close rate on fast replies and 15% on slow ones, and a $1,200 average job. Moving to 90% fast replies gives 100 × 0.70 × 0.15 = 10.5 extra customers a month, or about $12,600 in extra revenue a month. Your own close rates decide the answer, so measure them before you trust the number.

How do you measure your lead response time?

Take the last 20 to 30 leads. For each one, note when the inquiry arrived and when someone first replied, and subtract. Then count how many were answered within 5 minutes; that share goes in the calculator. Look at evenings and weekends separately, because that is where most small businesses lose speed. Research on online leads, such as the Harvard Business Review study on the short life of online sales leads, found that many companies take hours or longer to reply, which is why the share answered within minutes matters more than the average.

What is a good speed to lead?

  • Under 1 minute: possible with an automatic text or AI reply. This is the realistic goal for every lead, at any hour.
  • Under 5 minutes: a strong result for a person replying by hand during business hours.
  • 30 minutes or more: many leads have already contacted someone else or lost interest.
  • Next day: you are mostly reaching people who already booked elsewhere.

FAQ

What is speed to lead?

Speed to lead is the time between a new lead reaching out (a form, call, text or ad inquiry) and your first real reply. The shorter it is, the more of those people you reach while they are still interested.

How do you calculate lead response time?

For each lead, subtract the time the inquiry came in from the time of your first reply. Add those times and divide by the number of leads for the average. Also look at the median and at the share of leads answered within 5 minutes, because a few very slow replies can hide behind a decent average.

What is a good lead response time for a small business?

Within 5 minutes is a common target, and the closer to instant the better. An automatic text that goes out in seconds, followed by a call from a person, is the most practical way for a small team to hit it every time, including evenings and weekends.

How much revenue does slow lead response cost?

Use the calculator above: it multiplies the leads you could move into the fast group by the difference between your close rate on fast and slow leads, then by your average job value. Use your own close rates from your CRM or a month of records, not a statistic from someone else.

Are the numbers in the calculator real statistics?

No. The starting values are examples so the math is easy to follow. Replace them with your own lead count, close rates and job value to get a number that means something for your business.
